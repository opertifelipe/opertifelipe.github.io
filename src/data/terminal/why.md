# Why this website looks like a terminal

This website reflects two things I have been passionate about for a long time: **Linux and open-source software**.

I have always been fascinated by computers—by the possibility of understanding how they work, changing their behaviour and using them to solve difficult problems. Linux captures much of what I appreciate about computing: transparency, control, simplicity and the freedom to explore a system rather than merely consume it.

Despite that interest, I have never been as active in open source as I would have liked. This is probably connected, at least in part, to my background in physics. I reached professional software development through scientific research and computational problem-solving rather than through the more traditional path of computer science and open-source contribution. Nevertheless, my passion for computing has always remained a central part of my work and identity.

The terminal interface of this site is therefore not just a visual theme. It is a small tribute to the environment in which I feel most comfortable working, learning and experimenting.

## A deliberate trade-off

I am aware that this is not the most conventional or universally approachable interface. A command-line portfolio inevitably asks visitors to explore it differently from a standard website.

At the same time, I am not a famous person building a site for a broad public audience. I expect that most people who arrive here are interested in my technical work, my projects or my background—and are likely to be comfortable enough to navigate a terminal-inspired interface. Commands can be typed, but files and directories are also clickable, so knowing Bash is useful rather than mandatory.

This site is intentionally personal. It may not be for everyone, but it represents me far better than a conventional portfolio template would.
